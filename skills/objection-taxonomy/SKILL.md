---
name: Reading pushback
description: The six shapes a real objection takes, and what each one is actually about.
applies_to: always
---

Most pushback is one of six things. Knowing which one you are hearing changes what counts as
evidence for it, so read for the shape before you write the claim.

- **Money** — the number is wrong, or the shape of the spend is wrong. Listen for who has to
  approve it, what it has to displace, and what threshold it has to stay under. Those details are
  usually the real objection; the headline figure rarely is.
- **Authority** — the person on the call cannot decide alone. Listen for someone named who is not
  present, a committee, a board, a procurement step. What they need is usually ammunition, not
  persuasion.
- **Timing** — nothing is wrong except when. Listen for a freeze, a fiscal boundary, a migration in
  flight, a person on leave. Timing objections are the ones most often mistaken for disinterest.
- **A competitor** — someone else is in the frame. Listen for who, on what dimension they are ahead,
  and whether it was tried or only considered.
- **Trust** — they doubt the thing will work for them specifically. Listen for a past failure, a
  reference request, a pilot demand, a security or compliance step.
- **Staying put** — doing nothing is the real alternative. Listen for how the job is done today and
  what it would cost to stop doing it that way. This is the objection people voice least directly.

Two rules about the ones that matter:

An objection raised once in passing and an objection returned to twice are not the same fact. If the
call comes back to something, that is the blocker; say so only if the call actually returns to it.

Distinguish an objection from a question. "How does your pricing work?" is a question. "Your pricing
is the problem" is an objection. Recording questions as objections makes a healthy call look hostile.

Write each claim in the words the call used. This list is a lens for reading, not a vocabulary for
writing — a claim carrying words the speaker never said is a claim its own citation will not support.
