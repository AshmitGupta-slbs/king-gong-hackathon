---
name: Early discovery motion
description: What to listen for on a first or second call, before anything is being negotiated.
applies_to: stage:Discovery
---

Early calls are about whether there is a problem worth solving, not about terms. The useful signal
is what they do today and what it costs them.

Listen hardest for:

- **The current method.** How the job gets done now — a tool, a spreadsheet, a person, nothing. This
  is the real competitor on most early deals.
- **A trigger.** Something changed recently and that is why this call is happening: a hire, a
  failure, a growth step, a contract ending. If a trigger is named, it is the most important fact on
  the call.
- **Numbers they volunteer.** Team size, volumes, how long something takes, how often it goes wrong.
  Capture the figure exactly as said.
- **Who feels the pain versus who pays for it.** They are often different people, and on an early
  call you usually only meet one of them.
- **What they have already tried.** A previous failed attempt shapes everything and is often
  mentioned once, briefly.

Be conservative about intent on an early call. Interest expressed in a first conversation is
ordinary politeness as often as it is buying signal; read intent from what they have done, not from
how warm they sounded.

Next steps here are usually about access — to a person, to data, to a trial — rather than about
documents. Record them at the specificity the call gave them and no more.
