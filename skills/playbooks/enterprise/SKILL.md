---
name: Late-stage enterprise motion
description: What to listen for once a large deal is being negotiated rather than explored.
applies_to: stage:Negotiation
---

A deal at this stage rarely dies on the product. It dies in the machinery around the purchase, and
the machinery is what gets mentioned in passing and then forgotten.

Listen hardest for:

- **Who else has to say yes.** Names, roles, committees, boards. Someone appearing for the first
  time this late is significant, and so is someone who has gone quiet.
- **The steps between agreement and signature.** Security review, legal, procurement, vendor
  onboarding, a portal to register in. Each is a place the deal waits. Note who owns each one.
- **What the money has to displace.** Late-stage budget is usually taken from something else. If a
  line is named, that is a fact about the deal, not a detail.
- **Dates that belong to them, not to us.** Fiscal year ends, board meetings, renewal dates of the
  thing being replaced, someone's leave. These set the real deadline.
- **Reversals.** Anything agreed on an earlier call that is now being reopened. A reopened term is
  the single strongest signal on a late-stage call.

Next steps at this stage should name the artefact and its owner, because that is what the rep will
be chasing — a document, an introduction, a slot in a calendar.

Silence about the commercial terms on a late-stage call is itself worth noticing, but only report it
if someone actually raised and dropped the subject. Do not report an absence as an observation.
