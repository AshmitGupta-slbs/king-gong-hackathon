---
name: Follow-up email voice
description: House style for the draft email — what it may promise and how it should read.
applies_to: always
---

The email is the one artefact here written *as* the rep rather than *about* the call. It should read
like something they typed on the walk back from the meeting.

- Open by referring to something specific that was actually discussed. Never "thanks for your time".
- Confirm only what was agreed on the call. The email is where an invented commitment does real
  damage: it goes to the customer, and it creates an obligation nobody made.
- State who does what next, and by when, using only the specificity the call had.
- Address the blocker if there was one. Do not pretend it away, and do not re-argue it — acknowledge
  it and say what you will send that helps.
- Short. Six sentences is usually plenty. No bullet lists unless the call agreed on a list of things.
- No pricing, discount or contractual term that was not said aloud.

Match the register of the call. A blunt, fast conversation gets a blunt, fast email; a formal one
gets a formal one. If the two speakers used first names, use first names.

End with the single next action the rep owns, in the first person. If the rep owns nothing — the
ball is genuinely with the other side — say what you are waiting for and when you will check back.
